Pull the Goalie Sooner?

Every NHL coach who trails late has to decide when the goalie comes out. I tracked every pull in 6,700+ games since 2021 and built a model of the last ten minutes. The answer is yes, and by a lot.

What six seasons of pulls say

Does it hold up on games it never saw?

I fit every goal rate on 2021-22 through 2024-25 only, then scored the model on every 2025-26 and 2026-27 game where a team trailed by one or two goals at eight checkpoints in the last ten minutes. The model never sees outcomes; it only knows goal rates, the clock and when that team actually pulled.

How it works

  1. Data. Every regular season game from 2021-22 to today from the NHL's public feed (api-web.nhle.com). Each event carries a situation code that says how many skaters and goalies each side has, so I can see the exact moment a goalie leaves.
  2. Clean pulls. Delayed penalty extra attackers look like pulls in the data. Any goalie out stretch that ends with a penalty on the other team within 75 seconds is removed.
  3. Goal rates. Third period goals for and against per 60 minutes, split by score and by whether the goalie is in or out. Pulling down 1 lifts a team's scoring from 2.5 to 6.9 goals per 60, while goals against jump from 2.1 to 20.3.
  4. Model. A second by second Markov chain over the score and the clock, solved backwards from the final horn. In the regular season a tie is worth a point plus a coin flip in overtime, so it optimises standings points; the playoff mode optimises win chance.
  5. Uncertainty. 400 draws of every goal rate from its posterior, re solved each time, give the 90% range on the best pull time.
  6. Backtest and cost. Out of sample calibration as above, then each real decision is priced as points lost versus the best policy from that exact moment.

Robustness

Limits

The model treats both teams as league average unless you pick a team, ignores which zone the faceoff is in, and assumes the coach can pull at any second. Real coaches wait for possession or an offensive zone draw, which is one reason to start looking for the moment a little before the best time, not after it.
